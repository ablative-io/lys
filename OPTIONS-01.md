# Domain 01, shell and navigation: options per item

The reader is Cherie (rung 1, first MacBook this weekend) unless a line says otherwise. Each option says what she would see, which mistake from the reviews it avoids, and which of the page's purposes it serves (what it is for, what problem it solves, what value she gets). CONSENSUS.md decisions are applied, not re-argued.

## Item 1: the rail opens as ten unlabelled icons with names nobody can decode

Before: a 48 px strip of ten icons. The first is Operations (a diagram), and no word shows until someone finds the `[` key or the bottom icon. CONSENSUS fixes the names, the order and labels on by default. What is left to choose is how the words are drawn, and how much of the CONSENSUS order lands now.

- **A. Labels on, names changed, order kept.** Cherie sees words: Running agents, Dashboard, You, People and agents, Roles, Access, Access map, Secrets, Computers. *Avoids:* bare icons and abstract names (01 #1). *Misses:* she still meets Roles and Access map, rung-4 pages, and the first entry is still not Home. *Serves:* what it is for (each word says it), but not her problem, because the first thing she reads is not what waits on her.
- **B. Labels on, CONSENSUS names and order: Home, Waiting on you, People and agents, Running agents, Access, Computers, Apps, Secrets, then Settings, You, Help and Search at the foot.** Cherie reads Home first, then Waiting on you with its count, then her agents. Roles and Access map leave the rail but stay one key or one Search away. *Avoids:* bare icons (01 #1); IAM pages in the rail (STAKEHOLDERS: "they are wrong to be in the rail"); Home not being first (01 IA); Apps buried as the fourth Settings tab (01 IA). *Serves:* what it is for (words), her problem (what waits on her comes second, after Home), and value (the four things rungs 1 and 2 use are near the top).
- **C. The mock-up's 68 px rail: an icon with a 10 px word under it, always shown.** Cherie sees tiny words. "People and agents" and "Waiting on you" do not fit in 52 px and wrap to three lines of 10 px text. *Avoids:* bare icons, at no width cost. *Misses:* 10 px is below what a first-time reader reads comfortably. It is also an 18th font size (01 #10).

**Recommendation: B.** It is the only option that puts what waits on her before the pages she will never open, and it applies CONSENSUS as written rather than in part. Roles and Access map stay reachable by `g o` and `g h` and by Search, and I am asking domain 03 to add them as tabs of Access. "Waiting on you" leads to Requests, which already opens on "Waiting on me", until domain 03's single inbox exists. The page titles that still say "Network", "Operations" and "Graph" are asked of their domains.

**Built (B), looked at, second go.** In the first build every entry carried its g-key in a grey keycap. Seen at 1280 px, ten keycaps made the rail read like a cheat sheet, and the "g p" chip on People and agents was clipped against the edge. Cherie will never press `g p`. So the keys moved into the hover title, and only Help (`?`) and Search (`⌘K`, or `Ctrl K` off a Mac) keep a visible key; those are the two worth learning. What is still wrong: the pages under three of the new words still carry the old titles (Network, Operations, Graph, Dashboard). I have asked their domains for those exact lines, and until they land a rung-1 person clicks "Computers" and reads "Network". "Waiting on you" is still decided by the whole Access read, so a person who cannot read grants loses the entry. That is item 2, together with domain 03's ask. The foot holds five entries (Settings, You, Help, Search, Menu labels), which is one more than I would like. Once domain 05's sign-out lands, "You" becomes the person's name with Sign out beside it, which says more in the same space.

## Item 2: rail entries pop in one by one, and a failed check hides an entry without a word

Before: an entry is drawn only after its access read answers "ok". On every load the rail grows entry by entry, and the screenshot taken on the installed service showed two icons and a gap. A 500 or an unreachable service makes an entry vanish, so the page looks as if it never existed. "Waiting on you" is decided by four unrelated reads (domain 03's ask). CONSENSUS decides it: every entry drawn at once, refused ones disabled with the reason. The choice left is what "disabled" means.

- **A. Drawn at once; a refused entry is muted, keeps its link, and carries the reason in its title. Clicking it opens the page, which states the reason in full with a way back.** Cherie sees the whole rail from the first frame, and it never moves. Settings and Secrets are greyed; hover says "not open to you: only the administrator of this Lys changes these", and a click shows the same in a sentence. *Avoids:* pop-in reflow (01 #2), the silent vanish (01 #2), and a dead click with the reason hidden in a tooltip that touch and keyboard users never see (ranking #9: "tooltips' text made visible"). *Serves:* what it is for (the shape of the product is visible) and her problem (she learns why, in words, where she looked).
- **B. Drawn at once; a refused entry is a non-link span with `aria-disabled` and a title.** Cherie sees grey words that do nothing when tapped. *Avoids:* pop-in. *Repeats:* the reason lives only in a hover title, so on a phone or keyboard she gets no reason at all, which breaks "every error names the cause and the next act".
- **C. Drawn at once; refused entries gathered under one "Administrator" fold at the foot.** Cherie's rail shows four or five entries and one fold. This is closest to STAKEHOLDERS ("the rest is behind the administrator's door"). *Misses:* it re-decides the CONSENSUS order, and the fold's contents would differ by person, which is the same moving rail by another route.

**Recommendation: A.** It applies the decision as written, and the reason reaches every kind of reader. A 403 or a person who is not active is said as "not open to you" with the service's reason; any other failure (a 500, no answer) is said as "could not be checked", and the entry is still muted but opens normally. Search and the g-keys hide only a definite "not open to you". With it I apply domain 03's ask: Requests, Reviews and Drafts are each decided by their own read, and "Waiting on you" by Requests.

**Built (A), looked at, second go.** In the first build every marked entry was drawn the same way, half-transparent. Seen with Secrets refused (403) and Computers failing (500), they looked identical, and that is a lie: one is "not yours", the other is "broken, try again". Half opacity on muted text was also about 2.6:1, too faint to read as a link. So a refused entry now takes the `--muted-dot` token (about 4:1, still a link), and an entry that could not be checked keeps its colour and wears the `--warn` dot on its icon. Domain 03's ask is applied: Waiting on you is decided by `/requests` alone, and so are Reviews and Drafts by theirs. While writing the tests I found that the old "hidden Configuration" test passed while the check was still loading ("absent" is true before any answer arrives), so it now waits for the answer and asserts the mark. A control that put hiding back turned 10 of the 14 tests red. What is still wrong: the reason is in a hover title and on the page a click opens. On a phone the title never shows, so the click is the only way to learn it, and that page's words ("Ask the directory administrator to make the change") belong to the shared Gate, which domain 05 owns. At 420 px tall the rail squeezes its rows instead of scrolling (item 7).

## Item 3: "Network" means two things

Before: the rail's Network was the list of computers. The Running agents page has its own "Network" tab, a tree of running agents by computer, and the help called the computers "Computers". Item 1 renamed the rail entry, the Search row and the help sheets to Computers. Every "Network" a person still reads is in another domain's file: the canvas page's tree tab (`features/runtime/SessionCanvas.tsx:99`), the computers page title (`features/network/Network.tsx:124,166`), two Getting started links on Home (`features/dashboard/GettingStarted.tsx:94,107`) and a hint on the computer form (`features/network/ComputerAdmission.tsx:61`). In my folders, the numbered help layer still titles the computers page "Network", sends people to "Operations", and does not mention the tree view at all.

- **A. Asks only.** Write the exact lines for each owner, change nothing here. Cherie sees no change until those land. *Avoids:* help that runs ahead of the page. *Misses:* the help layer, which is mine, keeps calling the page by a word the rail no longer uses.
- **B. Asks, plus the help layer matched to the rail and describing all four views by what they show, not by the tree tab's label.** When Cherie presses `?` on the computers page, its heading says Computers, as the rail does. On Running agents, the help says "four views of the same running agents: by computer, Canvas, Sessions and the proxy", which is true whichever word the tab carries this week. *Avoids:* one word for two things (01 #3); help that leaves out a view (01 "Operations help"). *Serves:* what the page is for (each view explained) and her problem (the help agrees with the rail she used to get there).
- **C. B, and the help names the tree tab "By computer" now.** Cherie reads "By computer" in the help while the tab says "Network". *Repeats* the mistake this item is about: two words for one thing.

**Recommendation: B.**

**Built (B), looked at, second go.** With `?` on the computers page, the help heading now says Computers, the same as the rail. The page heading above it still says Network until domain 04's ask lands; that is the one place where the two words still meet, and only the owner of that page can end it. Looking at the same layer, I caught a false sentence I wrote in item 1: the rail's note said "Roles and the access map are under Access", but they become Access tabs only when domain 03's ask lands. It now says they are "found with Search", which is true before and after. Also seen, not fixed here: the layer's key table lists `j / k` twice, once from the page and once from the shell (item 8, one help).
