# Domain 01, shell and navigation: options per item

The reader is Cherie (rung 1, first MacBook this weekend) unless a line says otherwise. Each option says what she would see, which mistake from the reviews it avoids, and which of the page's purposes it serves (what it is for, what problem it solves, what value she gets). CONSENSUS.md decisions are applied, not re-argued.

## Item 1: the rail opens as ten unlabelled icons with names nobody can decode

Before: a 48 px strip of ten icons. The first is Operations (a diagram), and no word shows until someone finds the `[` key or the bottom icon. CONSENSUS fixes the names, the order and labels on by default. What is left to choose is how the words are drawn, and how much of the CONSENSUS order lands now.

- **A. Labels on, names changed, order kept.** Cherie sees words: Running agents, Dashboard, You, People and agents, Roles, Access, Access map, Secrets, Computers. *Avoids:* bare icons and abstract names (01 #1). *Misses:* she still meets Roles and Access map, rung-4 pages, and the first entry is still not Home. *Serves:* what it is for (each word says it), but not her problem, because the first thing she reads is not what waits on her.
- **B. Labels on, CONSENSUS names and order: Home, Waiting on you, People and agents, Running agents, Access, Computers, Apps, Secrets, then Settings, You, Help and Search at the foot.** Cherie reads Home first, then Waiting on you with its count, then her agents. Roles and Access map leave the rail but stay one key or one Search away. *Avoids:* bare icons (01 #1); IAM pages in the rail (STAKEHOLDERS: "they are wrong to be in the rail"); Home not being first (01 IA); Apps buried as the fourth Settings tab (01 IA). *Serves:* what it is for (words), her problem (what waits on her comes second, after Home), and value (the four things rungs 1 and 2 use are near the top).
- **C. The mock-up's 68 px rail: an icon with a 10 px word under it, always shown.** Cherie sees tiny words. "People and agents" and "Waiting on you" do not fit in 52 px and wrap to three lines of 10 px text. *Avoids:* bare icons, at no width cost. *Misses:* 10 px is below what a first-time reader reads comfortably. It is also an 18th font size (01 #10).

**Recommendation: B.** It is the only option that puts what waits on her before the pages she will never open, and it applies CONSENSUS as written rather than in part. Roles and Access map stay reachable by `g o` and `g h` and by Search, and I am asking domain 03 to add them as tabs of Access. "Waiting on you" leads to Requests, which already opens on "Waiting on me", until domain 03's single inbox exists. The page titles that still say "Network", "Operations" and "Graph" are asked of their domains.

**Built (B), looked at, second go.** In the first build every entry carried its g-key in a grey keycap. Seen at 1280 px, ten keycaps made the rail read like a cheat sheet, and the "g p" chip on People and agents was clipped against the edge. Cherie will never press `g p`. So the keys moved into the hover title, and only Help (`?`) and Search (`⌘K`, or `Ctrl K` off a Mac) keep a visible key; those are the two worth learning. What is still wrong: the pages under three of the new words still carry the old titles (Network, Operations, Graph, Dashboard). I have asked their domains for those exact lines, and until they land a rung-1 person clicks "Computers" and reads "Network". "Waiting on you" is still decided by the whole Access read, so a person who cannot read grants loses the entry. That is item 2, together with domain 03's ask. The foot holds five entries (Settings, You, Help, Search, Menu labels), which is one more than I would like. Once domain 05's sign-out lands, "You" becomes the person's name with Sign out beside it, which says more in the same space.
